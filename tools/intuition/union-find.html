Start with what you would do by hand

A class has 4 students, numbered 1 to 4. Friendships are announced one at a time: (1,2), (3,4), (1,3), (2,3). After each announcement you want to know: are these two already in the same friend group? For (2,3) the answer is yes: 2 is friends with 1, 1 with 3. That announcement is redundant.

By hand you keep a list of groups and merge two lists when a friendship connects them: {1,2} {3,4} → {1,2,3,4}. Merging means rewriting everyone's group label. With 4 students that is nothing. With a million users and a million friendships, relabelling a group of half a million people on every merge is far too slow.

Build the structure, one piece at a time

Step one: instead of a label, each student remembers one parent. A group is a tree, and the root of the tree (whose parent is itself) stands for the whole group. Start with everyone as their own root:

x1234
parent[x]1234

Step two, find: to learn someone's group, follow parents until you reach a root. find(2) = 2 at the start.

Step three, union: to merge two groups, find both roots and make one root the parent of the other. Nobody else is touched. After (1,2): parent[2] = 1. After (3,4): parent[4] = 3. After (1,3): parent[3] = 1. Now parent = [1, 1, 1, 3]. Announcement (2,3): find(2) = 1, find(3) = 1, same root, so it is redundant.

Step four, two small tricks so trees stay shallow. Union by size: attach the smaller tree under the larger, so a tree's height grows only when it at least doubles in size. Path compression: after a find, point every node you walked over directly at the root, so the next find is one hop.

Why it is correct

The invariant: two students have the same root if and only if a chain of announced friendships connects them. Union preserves it: linking root A under root B joins exactly the two groups whose members have roots A and B, and nobody else changes root. Path compression preserves it too: it changes parents but never which root a node reaches.

Why it is fast

With union by size, a node's root is at most log2 n hops away, because every time its depth grows by one its tree at least doubles:

height ≤ log2 n, so one find ≤ log2(106) ≈ 20 hops

Path compression then flattens the trees as you go, and the proven bound becomes α(n) per operation, the inverse Ackermann function, which is at most 4 for any input you will ever meet. A million operations cost a few million steps, against the trillions a relabelling approach could spend.

How to recognise it in an interview

Cues: "connected", "same group", "merge accounts", "how many provinces or components", edges arriving one by one with questions in between, "the first moment everyone is connected". The first thing to say: "I will keep a disjoint-set forest and check whether the two endpoints share a root." Redundant Connection is the example above: the first edge whose endpoints already share a root is the answer. Accounts Merge unions accounts that share an email, then groups by root. Number of Provinces counts how many roots remain.
