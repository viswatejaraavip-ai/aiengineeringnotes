Notes from building AI systems.

Retrieval, fine-tuning and evaluation — explained in plain English, with the real numbers, including the experiments that didn't work.

How to read these notes

The sidebar is a map of AI engineering in six sections: foundations, inference and reasoning, agentic AI, RAG, evaluation and ops, and security and governance. Each section has a few topics. Most of the notes written so far sit under one topic, RAG ingestion, chunking and indexing, in four groups: the core concepts, chunking patterns for real corpora, what changes when an index holds documents not everyone may read, and two systems built end to end with their measurements. LLM fundamentals has started with tokenization and the Transformer’s attention mechanism, and Inference scaling and serving with the KV cache, and Agent architectures, tool use and MCP with the ReAct loop. The other topics are listed so the shape of the whole is visible, and will fill in.

Read top to bottom if you are new to retrieval. Each page ends with a link to the next one in that order, and the sidebar ticks the pages you have finished, in this browser only. If you already build these systems, open the topic you need; every note stands on its own.

  1. Why attention has separate Q, K and V, and why it divides by √d_k Three projections let a token ask one thing, advertise another and hand over a third, which one similarity matrix cannot express. The dot product of two d_k-dimensional vectors has variance d_k, so without scaling the softmax saturates and its gradient vanishes. 24 Oct 2026 · 8 min read

Why memory is paid on the total count and arithmetic on the active count, with Mixtral and DeepSeek-V3 as examples, where the bandwidth advantage fades under batching, and what learned routing costs in training. 24 Oct 2026 · 9 min read

Inference scaling and serving

What it costs to run a model: where the time goes in a decode step, what memory is spent on, and the levers a serving stack has.

Serving fundamentals

  1. The KV cache, and why decoding is memory-bound Caching keys and values makes generation linear instead of quadratic, and leaves each decode step streaming the whole model and cache for one token's worth of arithmetic. The size formula, worked for a 70B model at 2k and 128k with and without grouped-query attention, and the mitigations: MQA, GQA, paged attention, cache quantisation. 24 Oct 2026 · 10 min read
  2. Grouped-query attention: the middle ground between MHA and MQA One key-value pair per query head, per group, or for all heads. Why sharing one pair per group shrinks the cache by the group ratio, keeps quality close to multi-head, and became the default in Llama, Mistral and Gemma, with the real head counts. How a trained multi-head model converts by mean-pooling and uptraining. 24 Oct 2026 · 7 min read

Decoding

  1. Temperature, top-k, top-p, and why T = 0 is not deterministic The sampling pipeline in the order it runs: temperature divides, top-k cuts by rank, top-p cuts by mass, then softmax and a draw. Temperature zero is argmax, and still varies run to run: floating-point sums depend on order, kernels change strategy with batch size, near-ties at the top flip, and the continuation diverges. 24 Oct 2026 · 9 min read

Agent loops

  1. Function calling: how the model decides, and what your code must do Tool definitions are prompt text; the decision to call is learned judgement; strict mode guarantees the arguments' shape but not their sense. Then the application's four steps, parse, validate and authorise, execute, return, with a worked order-lookup example and the mistakes that matter, starting with treating the model's arguments as permission. 25 Oct 2026 · 9 min read
  2. The ReAct pattern as a loop of function calls Thought, action and observation mapped onto a modern API: the reasoning before a call, the structured tool-call block and its stop reason, the tool-result message your code appends, and the exit when the model answers without a tool. A full loop in pseudocode, why structured calls beat parsing text, and what the loop needs that the paper did not cover. 25 Oct 2026 · 10 min read
  3. ReAct versus plan-and-execute, and the hybrid that production uses Interleaving reasons after each observation and wins when the next step depends on the last; planning first reasons before any and wins on known shapes, independent parts and long horizons. The plan-execute-replan hybrid: a planner without tools, executors running local ReAct loops in their own contexts, a replanner that revises what remains, with a walkthrough of a task that does not go to plan. 25 Oct 2026 · 11 min read

Tool design

  1. What makes a good tool schema, and why the description outranks the name The schema is a briefing the model reads. Name the action; spend the effort on a description that says what, when, when not, and what it costs; type every parameter, enum the fixed sets, require only what the function cannot run without. A weak schema rewritten, and why selection is decided by the description: a name has no room for conditions. 25 Oct 2026 · 9 min read

Core concepts

  1. Chunk size: recall falls, faithfulness rises What happens to a RAG system as chunks grow from 128 to 2,000 tokens: vector dilution hurts search while whole paragraphs help the answer, and why the fix is not a size in the middle. 19 Oct 2026 · 6 min read
  2. The chunk you embed is not the chunk you retrieve Search wants small text and the model wants big text. Decouple the indexed text from the retrieved text: parent-document retrieval, contextual embeddings, and summary or question indexes. 19 Oct 2026 · 7 min read
  3. Metadata on chunks, and the care needed when a model writes it What metadata buys a retrieval system, and the three things that go wrong when a model generates it at ingestion: hallucinated and drifting fields, cost, and injection from the documents. Constrain the schema, validate after, derive security fields from the system. 21 Oct 2026 · 10 min read

Chunking patterns

  1. How to chunk wiki pages Split on the heading tree, not on token counts. Prepend the page title and heading path, flatten infoboxes to key-value lines, fall back to paragraphs only in long sections, and retrieve the section when a paragraph matches. 19 Oct 2026 · 8 min read
  2. Semantic chunking made my retrieval evals worse Five reasons the switch from fixed 512-token chunks lowers MRR, NDCG and hit rate: variable length, lost overlap, tiny chunks starving the top-k budget, one global distance threshold, and ground truth labelled on the old boundaries. A four-step diagnostic that tells them apart. 21 Oct 2026 · 10 min read

Multi-tenant indexes

  1. Cross-tenant leakage in vector search, and how to test for it Four ways an ACL-filtered RAG still leaks between tenants: post-filtering after top-k, filters at the prompt layer, a shared semantic cache, and exposed scores. A canary and starvation-probe test plan that finds each one. 20 Oct 2026 · 9 min read
  2. Permissions on chunks, and keeping them in sync Put a stable document id on the vector, keep the ACL in a store built for change, pre-filter at query time from the auth token, and let the source system's webhooks or change data capture drive the sync. Filter on groups, not users. 20 Oct 2026 · 8 min read
